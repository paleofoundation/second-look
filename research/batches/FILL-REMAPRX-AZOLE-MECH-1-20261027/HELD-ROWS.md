## Hedgehog measurements by azole

Itraconazole: Cell reporter and mouse tumor experiments, plus small exploratory human studies. Itraconazole inhibited Hedgehog activity in cells and mouse tumors. Human pathway findings are mixed: a prostate study measured skin GLI1 changes, a lung study found no significant GLI1 or PTCH1 change in paired skin samples and no significant decrease in its tumor comparison against unrelated sample sets, and a five-person basal-cell-carcinoma study used sequential arsenic trioxide and itraconazole. Low clinical inference. A cell signal and an indirect or combination biomarker change do not establish cancer benefit or consistent inhibition in treated human tumors.
Sources: MECH-D07-HEDGEHOG-2010, MECH-D07-GLI1-LUNG-2020, P-D07-PROSTATE-2013, MECH-D07-GLI1-COMBINATION-2016

Ketoconazole: Shh-Light2 cell reporter assay. Kim 2010 Table S1 reports a Hedgehog reporter IC50 of 9.2 micromolar for ketoconazole, compared with 0.69 micromolar for itraconazole in that table. Low clinical inference. This weaker cell-reporter signal does not demonstrate a tumor effect in patients.
Sources: MECH-D07-HEDGEHOG-2010

Miconazole: Shh-Light2 cell reporter assay. Kim 2010 Table S1 reports a Hedgehog reporter IC50 of 12 micromolar for miconazole. Low clinical inference. The measured cell response is not human biomarker or cancer-benefit evidence.
Sources: MECH-D07-HEDGEHOG-2010

Clotrimazole: Shh-Light2 cell reporter assay. Kim 2010 Table S1 reports a Hedgehog reporter IC50 of 14 micromolar for clotrimazole. Low clinical inference. This cell observation cannot be transferred to patient tumor response.
Sources: MECH-D07-HEDGEHOG-2010

Fluconazole: Shh-Light2 cell reporter assay. Fluconazole showed no detectable Hedgehog inhibition in Kim 2010 under the tested conditions. Its Table S1 row has no numerical IC50. Low clinical inference. The negative assay result does not show that every azole inhibits this pathway, and it is not a test of fluconazole's established antifungal uses.
Sources: MECH-D07-HEDGEHOG-2010

Voriconazole: Shh-Light2 cell reporter assay. Chen 2016 detected no Hedgehog reporter inhibition from voriconazole up to 40 micromolar. Low clinical inference. This is direct negative cell-assay evidence, without human pathway-response data in this report.
Sources: MECH-POSACONAZOLE-HEDGEHOG-2016

Posaconazole: Reporter and cancer-cell experiments, mouse tumors and baseline human tissue characterization. Posaconazole suppressed Hedgehog-related signals and tumor growth in cell and mouse experiments. Chen placed its action at or near Smoothened, while leaving direct binding unresolved. Sun's human surgical samples measured baseline SMO expression, not a response to posaconazole treatment. Low clinical inference. These experiments do not establish human tumor target engagement or cancer benefit. Sun's mouse study also observed liver histology changes; nonsignificant enzyme comparisons do not establish safety.
Sources: MECH-POSACONAZOLE-HEDGEHOG-2016, MECH-POSACONAZOLE-RMS-2021

## Candida morphology measurements by azole

Itraconazole: Candida albicans isolates in hyphal-induction medium. Costa 2011 observed concentration- and strain-dependent reduction in hyphal formation with itraconazole. Resistant isolates retained more hyphae. Although some values differed by drug, the comparison with fluconazole was not statistically significant. Low clinical inference. In vitro changes in shape do not establish prevention of tissue invasion, symptom improvement or a patient treatment schedule.
Sources: MECH-AZOLE-HYPHAE-2011

Fluconazole: Susceptible and resistant Candida albicans isolates. Fluconazole reduced hyphal formation under specified laboratory conditions, but responses depended on resistance, concentration and exposure timing. Ha and White found that drug exposure during both growth and induction mattered in their tested isolates. Low clinical inference. Isolates taken from patients and then tested in a laboratory are not evidence that inhibiting hyphae improves patient outcomes.
Sources: MECH-D34-HYPHAE-1999, MECH-AZOLE-HYPHAE-2011

Ketoconazole: Three-dimensional Candida culture and filament susceptibility. Cagnacci 2008 found that ketoconazole reduced survival of filamentous Candida forms in a three-dimensional culture model. Killing existing filamentous cells is a different observation from preventing the initial yeast-to-hypha transition. Low clinical inference. This culture finding does not establish an animal hypha-directed dosing effect or patient clinical benefit.
Sources: MECH-KETOCONAZOLE-FILAMENTS-2008

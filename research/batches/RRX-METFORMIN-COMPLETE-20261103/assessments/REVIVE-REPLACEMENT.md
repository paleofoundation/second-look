# REVIVE: the corrected report of treatment for established long COVID fatigue

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test whether metformin or fluvoxamine improves fatigue in established long COVID. Adaptive trial with prespecified interim rules; the replacement restores a safety-driven dose amendment and the metformin futility stop omitted from the old report. The metformin comparison concerns treatment of fatigue after illness, not prevention during acute infection. The revised report, not the withdrawn original analysis, is the evidentiary unit.


## Who or what was studied?

399 adults in Brazil with new/worsening fatigue attributed to long COVID; 111 metformin, 150 fluvoxamine and 138 placebo. 22 outpatient sites, October 2023-February 2025; eligibility described fatigue 90 days to 24 months after infection and FSS at least 3. Metformin comprises 71 participants at 750 mg twice daily and 40 at 500 mg twice daily; all 111 remained in the intention-to-treat analysis. The two dose cohorts are sequential and not an independently randomized head-to-head dose comparison.


## What exactly was used?

Both metformin cohorts received the same extended-release formulation. 750 mg twice daily for the earlier cohort; 500 mg twice daily for later enrollees after the April 2024 amendment; 60-day treatment. Fluvoxamine was a separate randomized arm, not combined metformin treatment. Follow-up through day 90; no participant switched between the two metformin dose cohorts. The dose amendment must not be described as every treated person tapering to a lower dose.


## What was the comparator?

Matching placebo, shared within the platform. 1:1:1 allocation to active arms or placebo; eligibility could restrict allocation to metformin. Central web randomization with site stratification and variable blocks. Participants, site staff, investigators and outcome assessors were blinded; tablets and packaging were matched. Metformin stopped September 18, 2024; fluvoxamine continued until its later superiority stop in February 2025. Calendar-time and eligibility comparability of shared controls require attention when reviewing the detailed analysis code.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary: change in fatigue severity from baseline to day 60. Day 90 fatigue, quality of life and recovery were secondary. Fatigue Severity Scale: mean of nine 1-7 items; EQ-5D-5L for quality of life. Baseline, treatment to day 60, and follow-up to day 90. Patient-reported symptom and quality-of-life measures, not proof of viral clearance or reversal of underlying disease. The sample-size rationale targeted a 0.45-point FSS reduction; recovery was FSS below 3. A design target is not automatically a validated minimal important difference for every long COVID population.


## How large was the result?

Day-60 FSS treatment effects versus placebo: -0.02 (95% credible interval -0.48 to 0.44) for 750 mg twice daily and -0.04 (-0.63 to 0.54) for 500 mg twice daily. The dose cohorts contain 71 and 40 randomized participants respectively; the shared placebo group contains 138. Effects are FSS-point differences in change versus placebo; negative favors metformin. Day-90 estimates were -0.00 (-0.50 to 0.50) and -0.12 (-0.75 to 0.50). Overall recovery risk ratios were 1.09 (0.83-1.41) at day 60 and 0.96 (0.78-1.17) at day 90; quality-of-life contrasts also crossed no difference. These are Bayesian credible intervals; all metformin dose-specific primary intervals include zero and clinically relevant effects remain possible. The corrected conclusion is inconclusive. The null-centered estimates do not prove absence of benefit, and dose-cohort estimates are exploratory.


## What happened to the other observations?

One participant in each metformin cohort was lost after randomization; scheduled attendance was 70/71 and 39/40. Missing data were handled with 20 chained-equation imputations and pooling of posterior draws; placebo/fluvoxamine missed visits were also imputed. Gastrointestinal intolerance caused withdrawals and triggered the dose change; a complete individual adherence distribution is not provided in the main report. All randomized participants reportedly remained in the intention-to-treat analysis; attendance is not the analysis denominator. Intention-to-treat analysis with models adjusted for baseline FSS, site, age and sex. The participant is the unit; repeated FSS items/timepoints and dose analyses are not extra independent trials. Missing-at-random assumptions and the treatment of concurrent controls need verification against the unavailable companion code.


## What harms were sought and found?

Physical examination, vital signs and structured adverse-event queries at follow-up; CTCAE version 5 grading and blinded review of serious events. Table 5 uses 111 metformin participants (71 higher dose; 40 lower dose) and 138 placebo participants. Any adverse event: 49/111 (44.1%) metformin versus 49/138 (35.5%) placebo; dose cohorts 32/71 (45.1%) and 17/40 (42.5%). No grade 3/4 metformin events are tabulated. The DSMC reported a 9% gastrointestinal-event withdrawal rate prompting dose reduction; it was followed by a futility stop. 60 days of treatment and surveillance through day 90. The dose change is a consequential tolerability finding despite few severe events. Lower-dose tolerability cannot be proven superior by sequential cohorts.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Masking and randomized allocation are strengths; early stopping, dose amendment and original reporting errors require careful scrutiny. Between-dose comparisons are confounded by calendar time and different participants; Table 1 shows differences in vaccination and sites. The original report omitted changes that affected the metformin analysis. The replacement describes them; full protocol/code verification remains incomplete. Multiple outcomes and exploratory dose/subgroup analyses are not confirmatory evidence of responders. The small lower-dose cohort and early stop leave wide intervals around metformin effects. The main report excludes diabetes for metformin but Table 1 lists two lower-dose participants with diabetes; stated infection timing also does not neatly match all Table 1 ranges. These are retained as internal questions. The replacement fixes a major historical error without removing every remaining methodological uncertainty.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Adults with established long COVID fatigue treated in Brazilian outpatient settings. Extended-release metformin over 60 days; not equivalent evidence for the short immediate-release course in COVID-OUT. Fatigue and quality-of-life treatment, distinct from a new long COVID diagnosis after acute treatment. Other symptoms, longer follow-up, mechanistic effects and generalization to excluded populations remain uncertain. The guide must explain the prevention-versus-treatment distinction before comparing trial directions.


## Is this independent evidence?

S-REVIVE-2026 / NCT06128967. Original P-REVIVE-2026 DOI ANNALS-25-03959; notice ANNALS-26-03050; replacement ANNALS-26-02144. One trial with a withdrawn report and a corrected replacement, not an additional independent trial. Checked publisher notice, replacement and current guide inventory on November 3-4, 2026; no exhaustive new systematic search claimed. Keep the original identifiable for transparency while excluding its superseded efficacy analysis.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Gilmar Reis and the REVIVE Investigators, with Brazilian, Canadian, US and other collaborating institutions. Multicenter Brazilian outpatient trial. Latona Foundation funded the trial. Specific manufacturer/supplier details are not established from the main text inspected. Individual disclosure forms linked by the article have not been obtained; absence of conflicts is not assumed. The named funding source is recorded without treating it as a validity score.


## Is the source current and intact?

October 6, 2026 republication, DOI 10.7326/ANNALS-26-02144; not the March original. Complete publisher HTML and formal notice text were saved through normal browser access. Protocol recovered from the registry and key sections 2.1, 3.3.2 and 12 inspected; journal supplement, analysis code and separate final SAP remain unavailable. The English protocol has inconsistencies: section 2.1 identifies day 60 and a 1-point effect, section 12.5 says day 90, and power calculations use 0.45 points. Do not claim complete prespecification concordance. NCT06128967 current registry and posted English translation inspected. Cover identifies original amendment April 11, 2024, ethics approval April 14, 2024 and English translation April 7, 2026. Registration first posted November 13, 2023, after October 18 enrollment start. Protocol section 3.3.2 confirms dose reduction following 9% mild-event dropout. The formal notice says the dose reduction and futility stop occurred in the trial but were omitted from analysis/reporting; metformin is now inconclusive and fluvoxamine results are reported unaffected. Publisher inspection November 3, 2026; assessment November 4, 2026. Original quarantine is resolved to permanent exclusion of its superseded analysis. The replacement has a separate source-limited assessment, not inherited quarantine or invented approval.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

The guide's established-illness treatment statement must refer to the replacement and its inconclusive metformin result. Contradicts treating the withdrawn result as definitive proof that metformin does not work; also provides no demonstrated metformin benefit. Observed estimates and tolerability findings are separated from the authors' fluvoxamine conclusion and the editorial metformin interpretation. No efficacy statement may use the original report merely because it shares the same trial identity.


## What should this change in our understanding?

REVIVE does not establish metformin benefit for long COVID fatigue; its corrected finding is inconclusive. Dose-specific effects, tolerance, durability and responses in distinct long COVID phenotypes remain uncertain. An adequately powered, prospectively specified trial with stable dosing, transparent concurrent controls and complete source materials could change the conclusion. The original reporting error is resolved by version-specific handling, not by pretending the remaining evidence is definitive.


## Overall appraisal

The original report is permanently excluded from efficacy synthesis. The verified notice and replacement resolve why it was retracted and what changed. A substantive main-report appraisal is complete, but unavailable final SAP, code, journal supplement and disclosure files prevent labeling the full packet independently verified.

# Kidney safety review: historical context, superseded prescribing rules

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Review lactic acidosis risk in metformin users with impaired kidney function. Published search criteria; no review registration reported. Safety and possible prescribing-policy change. This is a 2014 review, not current labeling.


## Who or what was studied?

Human type 2 diabetes/CKD studies. MEDLINE and Cochrane, English-language January 1950–June 2014. 818 records to 65 reports: ten pharmacokinetic, twenty case series, thirty-one observational/surveillance, three meta-analyses and one trial. Report counts are not unique participant counts.


## What exactly was used?

Metformin; formulations/doses varied across studies. Oral clinical use and pharmacokinetic exposure. Other glucose-lowering treatment and concurrent illnesses varied. Heterogeneous study periods and follow-up. No single regimen or exposure is tested by this review.


## What was the comparator?

Other therapies, nonuse or kidney-function strata depending on study. Review-level study selection, not patient allocation. Predominantly nonrandomized evidence. No common masking method across included designs. Search completed July 2014. Observational associations cannot establish causal safety equivalence.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Lactic acidosis, lactate, metformin levels and clinical outcomes. Definitions varied; some code-based, some laboratory-confirmed. Person-year incidence in cohorts; short pharmacokinetic sampling. Serious clinical harm versus intermediate lactate elevations. Lactic acidosis definition: lactate above 5 mmol/L and pH below 7.35. Elevated lactate alone is not the same event.


## How large was the result?

Typical reported rates around 3–10 per 100,000 person-years; some higher. Cited meta-analysis: zero events over 70,490 metformin and 55,451 comparator person-years. Upper 95% incidence limits 4.3 and 5.4 per 100,000 person-years. Observational estimates conflict; one CKD analysis HR 6.37, CI 1.48–27.5. Sparse events and broad CIs limit conclusions for impaired kidneys. Zero observed events is not zero risk.


## What happened to the other observations?

Study-level attrition varied and was not pooled. Kidney function missing in more than 25% in one large cited cohort. Prescribing records do not ensure ingestion. Excluded small case series, nonhuman studies and non-English evidence. Narrative synthesis of heterogeneous reports. Primary study or cohort; overlapping databases possible. No de novo unique-person denominator.


## What harms were sought and found?

Clinical codes, spontaneous reports and laboratory diagnoses varied. No unified exposed denominator across 65 reports. Rare but potentially fatal acidosis; competing acute illness often present. No pooled withdrawal result. Study-specific durations. Detection and attribution are substantial limitations.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Confounding by indication, healthier-user selection and surveillance bias. Acute kidney injury, sepsis and hypoxia can cause both metformin accumulation and acidosis. Supplementary PRISMA details not separately acquired; no historical protocol audit. Narrative review, not a new pooled causal estimate. Rare-event estimates unstable in severe CKD. 2014 creatinine-based FDA restrictions and proposed dosing table are historical, not current policy. Do not copy old prescribing rules into the guide.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Diabetes with CKD, not healthy longevity use. Clinical metformin exposure; no evidence for experimental high-dose use. Supports kidney-function monitoring and contextualizes rare harm. Cannot establish safety during acute illness or eGFR below current contraindication threshold. Current product labeling supplies current precautions.


## Is this independent evidence?

Systematic review of mixed designs. Some shared national databases and nested reviews. Overlap must not be counted as independent trials. Search scope fixed at June 2014. Not a comprehensive 2026 safety update.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Silvio Inzucchi, Kasia Lipska, Helen Mayo, Clifford Bailey and Darren McGuire. Academic diabetes/clinical research authors. Investigator NIH/Yale support disclosed; no new trial funded. No product administration by reviewers. Multiple consulting, honoraria, travel and industry relationships disclosed. Disclosures do not determine the scientific conclusion.


## Is the source current and intact?

JAMA 2014 systematic review. Full text acquired. Separate supplemental flow diagram not acquired. Review protocol registration not reported. No correction identified in retrieved records; regulatory statements superseded by current labels. November 3–4, 2026. Use only as dated historical safety context.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Rare-harm interpretation and renal clearance. Contextual support, not authority to override current contraindications. Authors proposed expanded access; guide uses current FDA-label thresholds. No unsupported reassurance of zero acidosis risk.


## What should this change in our understanding?

Kidney function and acute illness are central to safe use. Rare-event risk in severe CKD remained uncertain in this review. Current high-quality safety data and updated labeling govern present clinical decisions. Do not individualize dose from this historical article.


## Overall appraisal

A useful historical synthesis with explicit limitations from rare events, heterogeneous definitions and confounding. It is unsuitable as current prescribing authority; current labeling supersedes its 2014 regulatory description.

# MASTERS: blunted muscle-mass gains, without a significant strength difference

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test whether metformin augments response to resistance training. Registry primary outcome is type II fiber area change; whole-body and thigh measures are separate outcomes. Hypertrophy and strength in older adults. Do not label every mass endpoint primary.


## Who or what was studied?

Community-dwelling nondiabetic adults, eligibility age at least 65; median age about 69. Kentucky and Alabama; independently mobile, predominantly White, generally high functioning. 109 randomized: 54 metformin, 55 placebo; 94 completed: 46 and 48. Not a trial restricted to frail adults with sarcopenia.


## What exactly was used?

Metformin tablets, 850 mg. One daily for 7 days then 850 mg twice daily, with 2-week wash-in and 14 weeks of training. Supervised progressive resistance training three days weekly in both arms. Sixteen weeks of medication, strength baseline after initial training adaptation. The comparison is exercise plus drug versus exercise plus placebo.


## What was the comparator?

Placebo with identical training. Parallel randomized allocation. Permutation details are in Appendix S5, not successfully acquired. Double-blind study. Concurrent treatment during the same training program. No metformin-only or no-training group was included.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Type II fiber area is registered primary; DXA, CT and strength are additional outcomes. Biopsy histology, DXA mass, CT area and dynamometry. Baseline and end of trial; strength change from week 4 to 16. Muscle structure and performance, not disability, falls or survival. No established clinically important threshold for the reported percent mass changes. Different measures of hypertrophy need not agree.


## How large was the result?

DXA total lean mass +0.41% metformin versus +1.95% placebo; thigh mass +0.45% versus +3.90%. DXA 46 versus 48; fiber area 30 per arm; corrected relative-strength analysis 44 per arm. Between-arm P=.003 for total lean mass, P<.001 for thigh mass; type II fiber area +14.5% versus +18.5%, P=.610. Knee extension strength +15.3% versus +23.1%, P=.055; corrected relative strength +14.8% versus +18.9%, P=.30. SDs rather than confidence intervals for many changes; lean-mass SD 2.25% and 2.69%. Mass attenuation is supported; a statistically significant strength loss is not.


## What happened to the other observations?

15/109 discontinued, including six due to adverse events. Observed-data completer analysis; endpoint samples smaller for CT and biopsies. Detailed pill/exercise adherence in inaccessible Appendix S1. Thirteen participants outside BMI target were randomized; five completed fewer than 37 sessions; selected high-quality biopsies analyzed. Analytic sample 94, not full ITT. Person; bilateral measurements and multiple fibers do not create independent participants. Completer selection and endpoint sampling limit causal interpretation.


## What harms were sought and found?

Adverse events collected; main report compares event occurrence. 109 randomized, with 94 completing; exact exposed safety denominator not separately established. More events with metformin, chi-square 9.82, P=.002. Six adverse-event discontinuations overall; full arm-specific event and withdrawal table unavailable. Sixteen weeks. Safety cannot be summarized as equal between groups.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Completer analysis, small mechanistic subsets and baseline sex/strength differences. Randomization helps; percent-change analysis does not remove all selection concerns. Registered primary fiber area result is null despite positive secondary mass findings. Many outcomes and tests without a global multiplicity adjustment. Modest sample; strength uncertainty includes no effect. Corrigendum replaces the relative-strength row and updates Appendix S2. Use corrected relative strength, not the stale original P=.188.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Generally healthy independently mobile older adults undertaking training. 1700 mg/day with exercise; not a direct test of intermittent self-dosing or other formulations. Training response, not treatment of established severe sarcopenia. Cannot infer that metformin cancels all benefits of exercise or shortens life. Scope the adverse interaction to the measured adaptations.


## Is this independent evidence?

MASTERS, NCT02308228. Corrigendum belongs to this trial; CP018 reviews it. No additional independent RCT is added by the correction or review. Current inventory reconciliation only. Keep MET-PREVENT separate because its population and question differ.


## Who conducted and supported it?

R. Grace Walton, Cory Dungan and colleagues. Universities of Kentucky and Alabama at Birmingham. NIA R01AG046920 and NCATS institutional awards. Product donation not established by the inspected main report. Authors report no conflicts. Funding disclosure is separate from scientific validity.


## Is the source current and intact?

Published Aging Cell 2019, DOI 10.1111/acel.13039, with 2020 corrigendum. Full report, actual correction and current registry acquired. Five article appendices not recovered through the attempted PMC URLs; posted registry protocol is available. Current NCT02308228 record confirms primary fiber-area endpoint. Correction changes relative strength: placebo 3.89 to 4.62 kg/kg, metformin 4.60 to 5.23; P=.30. November 3–4, 2026. The historical correction is incorporated, not merely noted.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Exercise adaptation and aging claims. Supports reduced gains in some muscle-mass measures; does not support improved strength. Authors emphasize blunted hypertrophy; editorial wording retains null primary fiber-area and nonsignificant strength findings. No general anti-aging efficacy inference follows.


## What should this change in our understanding?

Metformin did not augment resistance-training response in this trial. Long-term functional consequences and responses in other metabolic states remain uncertain. Larger prespecified functional trials with complete adherence and harms could clarify the balance. Exercise remains a cointervention, not an outcome to abandon.


## Overall appraisal

A useful randomized interaction study, limited by completer analysis and multiple outcomes. The registered primary fiber-area endpoint was null; DXA and CT support attenuation of some muscle-mass gains. The actual corrigendum is incorporated into relative-strength estimates.

# ALMERA: worse outcomes and more severe toxicity in a small randomized trial

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test concurrent and maintenance metformin with chemoradiation. Primary one-year treatment-failure proportion. Failure includes progression, death or discontinuation of trial treatment/evaluations. This endpoint is not conventional PFS.


## Who or what was studied?

Adults with unresected stage III NSCLC without diabetes. Seven Canadian institutions. 54 randomized: 26 metformin, 28 control; one per arm withdrew before treatment. Accrual was below the planned approximately 96 participants.


## What exactly was used?

Metformin supplied as oral study medication. Escalated to 2000 mg/day during chemoradiation, then up to 12 months. Platinum chemotherapy, mainly cisplatin/etoposide, plus 60–63 Gy radiation; later amendments allowed consolidation and durvalumab. Primary 12 months, survival follow-up to 24 months. Regimen differs from NRG-LU001.


## What was the comparator?

Chemoradiation without metformin. Central OCOG randomization, stratified by stage and later consolidation chemotherapy. Sequence-generation detail not fully provided in main report. Open-label; outcome adjudication committee masked. Recruitment 2014–2019; stopped for slow accrual. Stopping for accrual is distinct from an efficacy stopping rule.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary binary failure; conventional PFS/OS secondary. CT imaging and clinical evaluation; CTCAE 4.03 for grade 3+ events. Failure by 12 months. Clinical composite includes treatment feasibility as well as cancer events. No independently validated patient-important threshold for this composite. A discontinuation is not necessarily cancer progression.


## How large was the result?

Failure 18/26 metformin versus 12/28 control. 69.2% versus 42.9%; full randomized denominators. One-year conventional PFS 34.8% versus 63.0%, HR 2.42, CI 1.14–5.10. One-year OS 47.4% versus 85.2%, HR 3.80, CI 1.49–9.73. Wide intervals; primary Fisher P=.05. Reported failure-free difference −26.4 points has CI −51.0 to −0.9 when endpoints are ordered. The unfavorable direction is important despite limited precision.


## What happened to the other observations?

One pre-treatment withdrawal per arm; discontinuation counted in primary failure. Composite chosen partly to reduce informative censoring from inability to return. Only six completed one year of metformin. No randomized participant excluded from efficacy ITT. 54 for efficacy; 52 started treatment. Individual patient. Primary composite captures feasibility, while survival analyses answer separate questions.


## What harms were sought and found?

Only grade 3 or higher adverse events recorded. Table uses randomized denominators 26 and 28 although one per arm never treated. Any grade 3+ AE 14/26 versus 7/28; lung infection six versus zero, esophagitis five versus one; one grade 5 respiratory failure in metformin arm. Metformin stopped for toxicity in three; chemoradiation completion also lower in metformin arm. Most events occurred in first 84 days; follow-up extended beyond treatment. Do not compare these severe-event rates with all-grade rates from other trials.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Small, early-closed open-label trial, with masked adjudication. Metformin group had larger mean tumor volumes and fewer durvalumab recipients; chance imbalance may matter. Separate protocol and supplement not acquired; no prespecified stopping rule for accrual or efficacy reported. Several secondary survival comparisons; exploratory results require caution. Intervals wide but unfavorable. Main text sample-size target 94 versus abstract/planned 96; safety denominator differs from stated treated-population approach. Do not claim a proven biological mechanism for harm.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Nondiabetic unresected stage III NSCLC under intensive chemoradiation. Mostly cisplatin-based combinations with prolonged metformin. No support for routine anticancer addition; possible harm. Not a test of continuing medically indicated diabetes treatment or all cancer types. Population and regimen boundaries are essential.


## Is this independent evidence?

OCOG-ALMERA independent RCT. NRG-LU001 provides a separate null trial. Different participants and treatment duration. Current guide inventory only. Do not call the studies identical replications.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Theodoros Tsakiridis, Gregory Pond and colleagues. OCOG and Canadian oncology centers. CIHR grant; OCOG sponsored design, conduct, analysis and publication. Sandoz Canada and Teva Canada supplied metformin. Investigator grants, advisory fees and a family Roche employment/stock relationship disclosed. Supply and funding are recorded without inferring invalidity.


## Is the source current and intact?

Published JAMA Oncology 2021, DOI 10.1001/jamaoncol.2021.2328. Full text and tables acquired. Separate protocol and supplementary outcomes not recovered. NCT02115464 identified; historical registry not independently audited. No correction/retraction identified in retrieved records. November 3–4, 2026. Assessment is source-limited but primary findings are explicit.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Cancer treatment benefit and harm. Contradicts routine addition; supports highlighting severe toxicity and unfavorable survival. Trial observations do not establish the authors' speculative causal explanations. Do not bury harm under mechanistic enthusiasm.


## What should this change in our understanding?

This small trial raises a meaningful warning about this combination. Exact contribution of toxicity, treatment interruption and baseline imbalance is uncertain. Any future regimen requires direct controlled efficacy and safety evidence. The trial does not justify general claims that metformin promotes all cancers.


## Overall appraisal

Primary failure, conventional PFS, OS and severe toxicity all favored control. Small size and imbalances limit causal detail, but the results must be prominent in a guide considering anticancer use.

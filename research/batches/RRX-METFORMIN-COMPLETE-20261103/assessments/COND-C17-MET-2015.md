# Advanced pancreatic cancer: metformin did not improve survival

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test adding metformin to gemcitabine and erlotinib. Prespecified six-month overall survival. Clinical survival benefit in advanced pancreatic cancer. Mechanistic hypotheses are secondary.


## Who or what was studied?

Adults with advanced pancreatic adenocarcinoma. Four Dutch centers; approximately 73% metastatic; diabetes allowed. 121 randomized: 60 metformin and 61 placebo; diabetes in six and eight respectively. Not a prevention study.


## What exactly was used?

Oral metformin tablets. 500 mg twice daily in week one, then 1000 mg twice daily until progression or intolerance. Gemcitabine 1000 mg/m² on days 1, 8 and 15 every 28 days plus erlotinib 100 mg daily. Median follow-up 28.1 months. Not representative of every current pancreatic regimen.


## What was the comparator?

Matched placebo with identical cancer treatment. Central TENALEA allocation, stratified by hospital, diabetes and stage. Computer-generated blocks of six; block size concealed. Patients, physicians and study personnel masked. Concurrent randomized phase II comparison. Good allocation safeguards support the null comparison.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary six-month overall survival; PFS, response and metabolic analyses secondary. Survival follow-up and RECIST response; CTCAE toxicity. Primary six months. Clinical endpoints; insulin and drug concentrations exploratory. Design sought a very large six-month survival change from 50% to 75%. Power does not establish a small-effect equivalence margin.


## How large was the result?

Six-month survival 56.7% metformin versus 63.9% placebo. 60 versus 61 randomized; 52 versus 54 deaths over follow-up. Median survival 6.8 versus 7.6 months; HR 1.056, 95% CI .72–1.55. Six-month difference −7.2 percentage points, calculated from rounded estimates. Six-month P=.41; overall HR P=.78; PFS HR 1.18, CI .77–1.82. No demonstrated survival advantage.


## What happened to the other observations?

Follow-up continued after drug discontinuation. Serial plasma analysis available in selected participants, including 29 metformin recipients. Full-dose escalation in 38/60 metformin versus 49/61 placebo; dose reductions 34 versus 17. No reported postrandomization efficacy exclusion from ITT. Randomized ITT efficacy; selected biomarker subsets. Individual patient. Drug-concentration subgroups are not randomized dose comparisons.


## What harms were sought and found?

Graded treatment-emergent adverse events. 60 metformin and 61 placebo assigned. Grade 3/4 neutropenia 15 each; diarrhea six versus three; fatigue six versus two; no hypoglycemia reported. All study drugs stopped for toxicity in 13 versus eight; one metformin-only GI withdrawal and four metformin/erlotinib GI withdrawals. Median treatment 2.5 versus 4.6 months. Shorter exposure in metformin complicates direct toxicity-frequency interpretation.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Small phase II trial with good masking; limited exposure and dose reductions. More performance-status-2 participants and higher CA19-9 in metformin arm; adjusted survival remained null. Separate protocol/SAP not acquired for historical endpoint comparison. Biomarker and concentration analyses exploratory and multiple. Wide HR interval permits moderate benefit or harm. One Results sentence calls placebo n=60; randomization figure and total establish 61. Preserve the discrepancy; use coherent randomized denominators.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Advanced pancreatic cancer, including a small diabetic subgroup. This chemotherapy combination and conventional antidiabetic doses. Does not support a survival benefit as an add-on. Not all cancers, preventive settings or future combinations. Post hoc high-concentration survival cannot justify dose escalation.


## Is this independent evidence?

Kordes pancreatic phase II trial. Biomarker analyses are within this trial. No additional independent trial from serial drug measurements. Current guide sources only. One randomized family.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Sil Kordes, Michael Pollak, Johanna Wilmink and colleagues. Academic Medical Centre and other Dutch hospitals. Academic Medical Centre and Terry Fox Foundation; stated no funder role. Commercial product supply details not confirmed in inspected text. Authors declared no competing interests. Disclosure does not substitute for design appraisal.


## Is the source current and intact?

Lancet Oncology 2015, DOI 10.1016/S1470-2045(15)00027-3. Full report acquired from author-associated institutional source. Separate protocol and appendices not recovered. NCT01210911 identified in report. No correction/retraction identified in retrieved records. November 3–4, 2026. Primary results directly inspected.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Metformin as cancer treatment add-on. Contradicts expected major survival benefit. Trial data are null; mitochondrial or insulin explanations remain hypotheses. Do not imply a responsive high-dose subgroup was proven.


## What should this change in our understanding?

This regimen did not improve survival. Small effects, different regimens and biologically selected groups remain separate questions. Adequately powered randomized clinical outcome trials would be needed. Not a reason to change prescribed diabetes care without clinical review.


## Overall appraisal

Blinded randomized phase II evidence shows no survival benefit and more dose modification with metformin. Exploratory exposure-response observations cannot establish that higher doses work.

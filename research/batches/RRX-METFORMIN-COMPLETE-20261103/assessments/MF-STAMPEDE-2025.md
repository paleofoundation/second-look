# STAMPEDE: metabolic improvement without a demonstrated overall survival benefit

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test adding metformin to standard care for metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer. Primary overall survival; metastatic and nonmetastatic cohorts analyzed separately under SAP. Survival, progression and metabolic outcomes. This report covers the metastatic cohort only.


## Who or what was studied?

Men with metastatic prostate cancer without diabetes. 112 UK/Swiss hospitals; HbA1c below 6.5%, eGFR at least 45, performance status 0–2. 1874 randomized: 936 metformin, 938 standard care; median age 69; 94% newly diagnosed. Separate nonmetastatic participants are not added to this denominator.


## What exactly was used?

Oral metformin. 850 mg daily for four to six weeks then twice daily, continued if tolerated. ADT in all; 82% received docetaxel and only 3% an androgen-receptor pathway inhibitor. Median follow-up 60 months by case forms, 69 months including civil registration. Current treatment combinations may differ.


## What was the comparator?

Standard care alone. Central telephone allocation 1:1 with balancing factors. Minimization with 20% random component. Open-label. Concurrent randomized enrollment. Mortality is less subjective than symptom reporting, but treatment and adverse-event reporting were unmasked.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary overall survival; progression and metabolic endpoints secondary. Time-to-event models; laboratory measures and weight. Survival over follow-up; selected metabolic comparisons at 104 weeks. Survival clinical; glucose, lipids and weight intermediate outcomes. No universal patient-important threshold for the metabolic changes. Metabolic improvement does not establish prevention of cardiovascular events.


## How large was the result?

Deaths 453 metformin versus 473 standard care; median survival 67.4 versus 61.8 months. Randomized denominators 936 and 938. Overall survival HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.80–1.03, P=.15. At 104 weeks weight rose 2.00 versus 4.40 kg; adjusted contrast −2.48 kg in 583 participants. Weight contrast CI −3.55 to −1.41 kg; high-volume survival HR .79 but volume interaction P=.12. The high-volume subgroup does not establish a survival benefit confined to that subgroup.


## What happened to the other observations?

42 withdrew follow-up consent, 22 versus 20. Metabolic analyses use available subsets; baseline plus 104-week weight in 583. 897 confirmed metformin starters; 28 stopped within 30 days; median stopping time 39 months. 15 explicitly never started; another 24 assumed started because date missing. ITT efficacy; 921 metformin safety versus 938 control. Individual participant. 26 control participants started metformin; no pill-diary adherence measure.


## What harms were sought and found?

Routine adverse-event and serious-event reporting; CTCAE grading. 921 metformin versus 938 standard care. Grade 3+ events 523/921 versus 487/938; any diarrhea 600 versus 350; grade 3 diarrhea 46 versus 29. 134 of 510 who stopped metformin cited excessive toxicity. Serious events collected to progression in control and until progression plus at least 30 days after stopping in metformin. Different observation windows and prolonged exposure affect harm comparisons.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Open-label design, missing metabolic measurements and treatment crossover. Randomization supports overall contrast; availability-selected metabolic subsets may differ. SAP version 4.0 dated March 19, 2024 inspected for primary and subgroup rules. Many secondary/subgroup comparisons; high-volume PFS interaction is exploratory. Overall survival interval includes no effect and modest benefit. Appendix contents call protocol v21 but embedded protocol is v23 dated October 5, 2021. The appendix naming discrepancy is retained; it is not evidence of a different result.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Metastatic hormone-sensitive prostate cancer without diabetes. Long-term oral metformin with predominantly ADT plus docetaxel. No overall survival benefit demonstrated; less weight gain observed. Very few received contemporary AR-pathway inhibitors; no direct generalization to every regimen. Not evidence to replace established cancer treatment.


## Is this independent evidence?

STAMPEDE metformin comparison, arm K. Other platform arms and nonmetastatic cohort are separate comparisons. Shared platform does not make all publications independent metformin trials. Current guide inventory, not exhaustive prostate-cancer synthesis. Count this randomized comparison once.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Silke Gillessen and STAMPEDE collaborators. MRC Clinical Trials Unit and participating UK/Swiss centers; UCL sponsor. Cancer Research UK, Prostate Cancer UK and UKRI/MRC; funder approved design/amendments, not analysis. Product supply not confirmed in this targeted assessment. Numerous disclosed investigator industry relationships; no inference of invalidity from funding alone. The primary estimate governs the guide conclusion.


## Is the source current and intact?

Lancet Oncology 2025, DOI 10.1016/S1470-2045(25)00231-1. Full report and appendix acquired. Protocol and SAP included; targeted primary, subgroup and safety sections inspected, not every platform appendix page. NCT00268476 and ISRCTN78818544. No correction/retraction identified in retrieved records. November 3–4, 2026. Do not label the targeted audit a complete audit of all platform documents.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Survival versus metabolic outcomes. Supports less weight gain; does not establish overall survival benefit. Observed metabolic results and authors’ subgroup hypotheses are presented separately. No favorable subgroup headline.


## What should this change in our understanding?

Metformin improved selected metabolic measures but did not significantly improve overall survival. Effects with current regimens and prospectively confirmed subgroups remain uncertain. Direct trials with modern therapy and clinical outcomes could change the conclusion. Treatment decisions require the cancer team.


## Overall appraisal

Large randomized evidence does not demonstrate overall survival improvement in the metastatic cohort. Metabolic effects are real intermediate findings in a smaller available-case subset. The high-volume survival subgroup lacks a convincing interaction and cannot overturn the primary result.

# MA.32 metabolic companion: improved biomarkers without established cancer benefit

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Measure weight and metabolic effects of metformin and possible SNP modification. Planned companion analyses; primary parent endpoint was invasive disease-free survival. Six-month metabolic response. This report predates the definitive cancer-outcome report.


## Who or what was studied?

Nondiabetic adults with high-risk operable breast cancer. International MA.32 participants still taking study drug with paired fasting bloods. 3649 randomized; metabolic subset 2915: 1412 metformin, 1503 placebo; SNP subset 2747. 734 randomized participants are absent from the metabolic cohort.


## What exactly was used?

Metformin 850 mg caplets; matching placebo. Once daily for four weeks then twice daily; intended five years. Standard surgery and systemic/radiation cancer therapies. Baseline to six months in this analysis. Different follow-up from the final cancer trial.


## What was the comparator?

Placebo. Parent 1:1 assignment. Computer-generated randomization. Double-blind; laboratory assays blinded to allocation. Paired baseline and on-treatment sampling. Selection after randomization limits a simple ITT interpretation.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Weight, glucose, insulin, HOMA, leptin, hsCRP; interaction analyses. Central insulin/leptin/hsCRP assays with 10% repeats; local glucose. Six months. Intermediate metabolic measures, not cancer recurrence. Clinical-benefit thresholds not established for these changes. No surrogate validation for anticancer benefit.


## How large was the result?

Median weight change −1.4 kg versus +.5 kg. Weight-change n=1394 versus 1499; metabolic subset n=1412/1503. Adjusted metformin/placebo ratios: insulin .85, HOMA .83, leptin .80, hsCRP .84. Weight standardized ratio .97, CI .97–.98; ratios adjust baseline, age and BMI. Reported P<.0001 for these comparisons; no qualitative SNP interaction. Raw within-arm percentages and adjusted between-arm ratios are different quantities.


## What happened to the other observations?

Postrandomization exclusion of those without paired on-treatment samples. More metformin-assigned and Black participants excluded; diet/activity missing in over 75%. Required on-drug status, but short-term dosing compliance uncertain. 412 metformin and 322 placebo excluded from metabolic cohort. Available paired samples, not full randomized ITT. Individual participant. Selection may distort effect magnitude and generalizability.


## What harms were sought and found?

No new comprehensive safety analysis in this paper. Selected metabolic cohort is not an unbiased safety denominator. Authors state toxicity/compliance data were unavailable until efficacy analysis. Drug-related discontinuation may contribute to exclusions. Six-month biomarker window. Use the later parent report for harms, retaining its numerical discrepancies.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Conditioning on on-treatment samples and multiple biomarkers. Randomization helps, but selection and unmeasured lifestyle changes remain. Parent primary clinical endpoint explicitly deferred. Multiple biomarker and interaction tests without a stated family-wise correction. Large biomarker sample, but no clinical-outcome estimate here. Printed HOMA formula units are inconsistent with conventional dimensional form; do not use it as a calculation instruction. Metabolic findings cannot validate a cancer-prevention claim.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Selected nondiabetic breast-cancer population. 1700 mg/day after ramp-up. Supports metabolic change in this setting. Not evidence of survival benefit, healthy-person longevity or a validated genetic prescribing rule. Later clinical results take precedence for clinical claims.


## Is this independent evidence?

CCTG MA.32. Final COND-C15-MA32-2022 and earlier 492-person safety analysis. Same trial, overlapping samples. Current family reconciliation. Do not count as an independent positive cancer trial.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Pamela Goodwin, Bingshu Chen, Marguerite Ennis and colleagues. CCTG and central Toronto laboratories. Public/cancer-charity support; statement of no sponsor role in this report. Apotex in-kind metformin/placebo. Roche employment and several oncology-industry grants/honoraria disclosed. Funding statements are report-specific.


## Is the source current and intact?

npj Breast Cancer 2021, DOI 10.1038/s41523-021-00275-z. Full XML and supplements acquired. Supplement 1 is an interaction table; supplement 2 is a reporting summary, not the full trial protocol/SAP. NCT01101438, first posted April 12, 2010 according to report. No correction identified in retrieved records; clinical follow-up published in 2022. November 3–4, 2026. Corrects the earlier mistaken classification of these files as protocol/SAP.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Biomarkers versus cancer outcomes. Metabolic improvement supported, anticancer benefit not established. Authors anticipated possible clinical benefit; later primary report was negative. Do not preserve an outdated “outcomes forthcoming” statement.


## What should this change in our understanding?

Improved insulin and weight measures did not establish improved cancer outcomes. Mechanistic mediation and selected subgroups require direct testing. Clinical outcomes, not biomarker changes alone, could change treatment conclusions. One trial can improve biomarkers while failing its clinical endpoint.


## Overall appraisal

A large but selected paired-sample analysis supports metabolic effects. It is not independent cancer-efficacy evidence, and the subsequent negative clinical endpoint prevents interpreting improved biomarkers as proven cancer benefit.

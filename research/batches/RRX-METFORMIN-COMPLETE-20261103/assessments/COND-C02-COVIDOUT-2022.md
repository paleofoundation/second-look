# COVID-OUT acute report: negative primary endpoint and promising secondary findings

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Prevent severe acute COVID-19 with early outpatient medication. Prespecified composite through day 14. Hypoxemia, emergency visit, hospitalization or death. Secondary endpoints do not replace the primary.


## Who or what was studied?

Adults 30–85 with overweight or obesity and recent COVID-19. Six US institutions, remote enrollment; positive test within three days and symptoms within seven. 1431 randomized; 1323 mITT: 663 metformin, 660 control; 52.2% vaccinated. Median age 46; limited applicability to current immune backgrounds.


## What exactly was used?

Immediate-release metformin. Oral escalation to 1500 mg/day over six days, 14 days total. Factorial fluvoxamine, ivermectin or matched placebo assignments. Primary day 14; hospitalization/death also day 28. No comparison of IR versus ER.


## What was the comparator?

Concurrent matched placebo for metformin. Equal probability among open groups, site-stratified. Pregenerated mass-weighted urn sequence. Blinded participants, clinicians and outcome assessors; similar pill burden. December 2020–January 2022, with factorial expansion May 2021. Comparisons between different drugs’ crude rates are invalid because enrollment periods differ.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary four-part composite; health-care component and symptoms secondary. Home oxygen saturation, medical-record verification and symptom diaries. Day 14 primary. Composite mixes a noisy physiologic measure with major clinical events. Planned 35% relative reduction, not a validated universal MCID. Oximeter limitations weaken the endpoint but do not justify retrospectively calling the trial primary-positive.


## How large was the result?

Primary 154/652 metformin versus 179/653 control; emergency visit/hospitalization/death 27/652 versus 48/655. Full mITT denominators 663/660; displayed event fractions are observed complete cases. Adjusted primary OR .84, 95% CI .66–1.09, P=.19. Secondary health-care composite OR .58, CI .35–.94; day-14 hospitalization/death OR .47, CI .20–1.11. Day-28 hospitalization/death 8/596 versus 19/601; secondary CIs not adjusted for multiplicity. No primary benefit demonstrated; secondary signals warrant further testing.


## What happened to the other observations?

108 excluded from mITT because no trial drug received. Primary follow-up missing about 1.4%; symptom diary missing about 25%. Drug discontinuation recorded; current registry contains six factorial-arm counts. ITT sensitivity analyses described separately from mITT. mITT with multiple imputation; symptoms not imputed. Individual participant. Factorial arms are not independent trials.


## What harms were sought and found?

Non-COVID events solicited through day 14; serious events through day 28 in registry. Registry six-arm safety populations total 1323. No medication-related serious event reported; one all-cause death occurred in a metformin/ivermectin recipient. Metformin-only discontinuations 56/284 versus double-placebo 49/295; combination-arm counts differ. Short treatment and acute follow-up. These arm-specific discontinuations are not the entire factorial metformin effect.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Remote oximetry, mITT exclusions and missing symptom diaries. Concurrent randomization and adjusted co-medication/vaccination analyses reduce confounding. Registered protocol/SAP recovered; report explicitly identifies secondary analyses. No multiplicity correction for secondary outcomes. Hospitalization/death events rare with wide intervals. Wording about symptom reduction is awkward in Results; figures and conclusion indicate no symptom benefit. Use the explicit conclusion and endpoint hierarchy.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Early acute outpatient infection in overweight/obese adults. Fourteen-day IR course. Not established long-COVID treatment or a comparison with authorized antivirals. Variants, vaccination and enrollment criteria limit extrapolation. Long-term follow-up is a separate outcome in the same trial.


## Is this independent evidence?

COVID-OUT randomized family. P-COVIDOUT-2023 long-term and MF-COVID-VIRAL-2024 virologic reports. Overlapping participants; count once. Current inventory and linked registry. Multiple reports increase outcome detail, not independent replication.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Carolyn Bramante and COVID-OUT collaborators. US investigator-led multicenter remote trial. Parsemus, Rainwater, Fast Grants and UnitedHealth Group Foundation; stated no funder role. Apotex fluvoxamine placebo; Edenbridge ivermectin active/placebo; metformin donor not established here. Separate original NEJM disclosure forms not recovered; later related report provides named investigator disclosures. Do not substitute later disclosures for exact original forms.


## Is the source current and intact?

NEJM 2022, DOI 10.1056/NEJMoa2201662. Full text, tables and current trial record inspected. Protocol/SAP recovered; original acute supplement not fully audited. NCT04510194. Long-term 2023 correction belongs to the companion report, not an acute primary-result correction. November 3–4, 2026. Preserve report-specific version history.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Acute severity and symptom claims. Primary null; secondary clinical benefit signal. Authors’ cautious primary conclusion is retained despite stronger later commentary. No claim of proven general antiviral replacement.


## What should this change in our understanding?

Early metformin did not significantly reduce the original primary composite. Whether secondary benefits generalize remains uncertain. Independent current-population trials with prespecified clinical endpoints matter. Guide separates this from long-COVID prevention.


## Overall appraisal

A well-blinded factorial trial with a negative primary outcome, a noisy oximetry component and favorable but multiplicity-unadjusted secondary outcomes. It is one trial family shared with the viral-load and long-term reports.

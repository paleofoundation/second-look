# Post-polypectomy adenomas: a promising small trial, not proof of cancer prevention

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test low-dose metformin against recurrence of colorectal adenomas and polyps. Coprimary number and prevalence at one year. Post-polypectomy lesion recurrence. Not colorectal cancer incidence or mortality.


## Who or what was studied?

Nondiabetic adults aged 40–80 after polypectomy. Five Japanese hospitals; excluded regular NSAIDs, hereditary syndromes and major organ disease. 498 screened; 151 randomized, 79 metformin and 72 placebo; 133 follow-up colonoscopies, 71 and 62. About 70% had multiple or advanced adenomas at baseline.


## What exactly was used?

Metformin tablets versus lactose placebo. Oral 250 mg once daily after breakfast for one year. Prior polypectomy; surveillance colonoscopy. One year. This unusually low research dose is not a prescribing recommendation.


## What was the comparator?

Identical placebo. Central stratification by hospital, age, sex and BMI. Computer-generated random allocation. Patients, clinicians, endoscopists and investigators masked. Concurrent 2011–2014 recruitment. Experienced endoscopists and masked video review strengthen outcome ascertainment.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Coprimary lesion count and prevalence; metabolic measures secondary. Colonoscopy, three-expert video review and pathology. One-year repeat examination. Adenoma/polyp recurrence is an intermediate cancer-risk endpoint. No established patient-important threshold for one-year lesion reduction. Adenoma reduction is not itself cancer prevention.


## How large was the result?

Any polyp 27/71 metformin versus 35/62 placebo; adenoma 22/71 versus 32/62. Observed colonoscopy denominators 71 and 62, not all 151 randomized. Polyp RR .67, 95% CI .47–.97; adenoma RR .60, CI .39–.92. Any-polyp prevalence 38.0% versus 56.5%; median polyp count zero versus one. P=.034 for polyp prevalence, .016 for adenoma prevalence; count comparisons P=.041 and .037. Adenoma percentage printed as 30.6% does not reproduce 22/71; retain counts and reported RR.


## What happened to the other observations?

Eight metformin and ten placebo lacked follow-up colonoscopy. Complete-case outcome analysis despite ITT terminology. Median compliance 91% versus 92%; nobody removed for under-80% adherence. 347 excluded before randomization, including 183 with concern about incomplete baseline clearance. 133 with follow-up colonoscopy. Individual participant; multiple lesions are not extra patients. Missing outcome bias remains possible.


## What harms were sought and found?

NCI-CTCAE version 4.0 grading during follow-up. Safety table uses 71 and 62 completers. Six metformin and nine placebo events listed, all grade 1; diarrhea one versus four; no serious events reported. One placebo discontinuation for diarrhea. One year. Small size and completer safety denominator cannot establish absence of rare harm.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Small regional trial, incomplete colonoscopy follow-up and possible missed baseline lesions. Randomization limits confounding; post hoc insulin-resistance groups are not randomized. Separately published 2012 protocol not acquired in this packet. Multiple coprimary/count/metabolic tests without a reported multiplicity adjustment. Modest event counts, confidence intervals near no effect. Reported adenoma 30.6% differs from 22/71; claimed ITT actually depends on observed follow-up. HOMA-IR subgroup interaction P=.141 does not prove a predictive responder marker.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Selected Japanese nondiabetic post-polypectomy adults. 250 mg/day, one year. Supports possible reduction in recurrent lesions. Not average-risk adults, longer-term cancer prevention, cancer treatment or all doses. Surveillance remains a separate established care question.


## Is this independent evidence?

Higurashi 2016 randomized trial. Hosono 2010 crypt-foci pilot is a separate earlier study. Distinct participants; same research lineage does not mean independent replication of cancer outcomes. Current guide inventory only. Do not pool crypt foci, adenomas and cancer as one outcome.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Takuma Higurashi, Atsushi Nakajima and colleagues. Five Japanese hospitals. Japanese Ministry of Health, Labour and Welfare cancer-research grant; stated no funder role. Drugs purchased from Dainippon Sumitomo and Kokando. Authors declared no competing interests. Purchased products are not described as donated.


## Is the source current and intact?

Lancet Oncology 2016, DOI 10.1016/S1470-2045(15)00565-3. Full nine-page article acquired and read. Separate protocol and any additional appendix not recovered. UMIN000006254 identified. No correction/retraction identified in retrieved records. November 3–4, 2026. The numerical discrepancy remains visible in the assessment.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Adenoma recurrence versus colorectal cancer prevention. Supports a preliminary lesion-reduction signal; cancer prevention remains unproven. Authors’ broader chemoprevention interpretation exceeds the measured one-year cancer outcomes. No colorectal cancers detected at follow-up does not prove prevention.


## What should this change in our understanding?

A small blinded trial is encouraging for recurrent lesions. Durability, generalizability, rare harms and cancer outcomes remain uncertain. Larger independent long-term randomized trials with cancer outcomes could strengthen the case. Do not recommend self-treatment or reduced surveillance.


## Overall appraisal

Promising randomized evidence for one-year lesion recurrence in a selected population, limited by sample size, missing colonoscopies, multiplicity and surrogate outcomes. It does not establish reduced colorectal cancer incidence or mortality.

# Complex I experiments: a proposed mechanism with exposure and model limits

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test whether complex I inhibition explains metformin effects on gluconeogenesis. No preregistered protocol reported. Mitochondrial respiration and liver metabolites. Not a trial of repurposed clinical efficacy.


## Who or what was studied?

Male Wistar rats, rat H4IIE hepatoma cells and isolated mitochondria/particles. Rats 250–300 g; hepatocytes/liver mitochondria often from fasted animals. Typically three independent preparations; four livers per metabolite group; other assays report separate n. No human participants.


## What exactly was used?

Sigma metformin; phenformin comparison. Cells 50/100 µM for 24/60 h; isolated preparations millimolar doses; rats 50/150 mg per rat daily five days. Substrate, ADP and glucagon manipulations; digitonin permeabilization. Minutes/hours in isolated assays, five-day animal exposure. Per-rat doses are not human doses.


## What was the comparator?

No-drug/water controls and alternative mitochondrial substrates. Preparation-level paired comparisons where stated. Animal allocation randomization not reported. Blinding not reported. Time- and concentration-dependent comparisons. Mechanistic controls improve specificity but do not establish clinical transportability.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Respiration, NADH oxidation, glucose production and metabolite ratios. Oxygen electrode, spectrophotometry and metabolite assays. Assay-dependent timepoints. Biochemical outcomes, not patient outcomes. No human MCID. Calculated free ATP/ADP differs from measured total nucleotide levels.


## How large was the result?

50 µM metformin inhibited glutamate/malate oxidation by 12.6±4.3% at 24 h and 29.4±7.7% at 60 h. Figure-specific independent preparations; control cell oxidation n=18 glutamate/malate and n=15 succinate. Mean±SEM; succinate oxidation unaffected. Submitochondrial particles required about 79 mM for half inhibition. No clinical CI; paired/unpaired t-tests by experiment. Different membrane preparations require very different exposures.


## What happened to the other observations?

Animal attrition not tabulated. Missing experiments and exclusions not fully documented. Oral dosing stated; no direct plasma-exposure measurement for every study group. Isolation changes membrane permeability and may lose accumulated drug. Independent preparations or animals by assay. Biological preparation/animal, not repeated instrument readings. Assay artifacts are discussed by the authors.


## What harms were sought and found?

Visible distress and body weight observed. Small short-duration rat groups. No visible distress or significant mean weight effect reported. Toxicity-related withdrawals not reported. Five days. Not a human safety assessment.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Small mechanistic experiments, unclear allocation/masking and isolation artifacts. Substrate controls distinguish respiratory-chain sites; other cellular actions remain possible. No preregistered endpoint hierarchy. Numerous biochemical comparisons. Limited biological n and no human clinical estimate. Free ATP/ADP values are calculated; total ATP was not significantly changed. Do not present calculated energetic changes as directly measured human effects.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Rat liver/cell biology. Some cell exposures micromolar, many isolated assays millimolar; human liver accumulation inferred. Supports a complex-I mechanism hypothesis. Does not prove it is the sole mechanism, or demonstrate cancer, infection or longevity benefit. Model/exposure boundaries are central.


## Is this independent evidence?

Owen, Doran and Halestrap experimental program. El-Mir 2000 cited as a related independent report with a different direct/indirect interpretation. Multiple preparations within this paper are not separate clinical trials. Scoped mechanism sources, not exhaustive mechanistic review. The unacquired El-Mir full text is not silently counted as assessed.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Mark R. Owen, Elena Doran and Andrew P. Halestrap. University of Bristol biochemistry. British Diabetic Association. Commercial Sigma reagents. No modern structured competing-interest declaration located. Absence of a declaration is not a declaration of no conflicts.


## Is the source current and intact?

Biochemical Journal 2000;348:607–614. Complete publisher-formatted PDF recovered from a public mirror and title/authors/pages verified. No separate supplement relied upon. Nonclinical study; Home Office animal compliance stated. No correction/retraction identified in retrieved records. November 4, 2026. Mirror provenance retained with SHA-256.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Cellular energy and complex I. Indirect mechanistic support. Observed substrate-specific inhibition; authors infer mitochondrial accumulation and clinical relevance. No repurposing benefit follows automatically.


## What should this change in our understanding?

Complex I inhibition is one experimentally supported proposed mechanism. Relative contribution in human tissues at ordinary exposure remains a separate question. Human target-engagement and outcome studies needed for clinical claims. No dose recommendation.


## Overall appraisal

Substrate-specific and concentration/time-dependent experiments support a complex-I hypothesis. Isolation artifacts, high assay concentrations and small nonhuman samples limit extrapolation; clinical repurposing efficacy is not tested.

# Parkinson disease: adjunctive metformin pilot

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test adjunctive metformin in Parkinson disease receiving levodopa/carbidopa. The report identifies UPDRS improvement as primary and circulating biomarkers as secondary; registration NCT05781711 is given. Clinical symptom improvement, with biological hypotheses evaluated separately. A biomarker finding cannot replace the primary clinical comparison.


## Who or what was studied?

Human adults at least 50 years old with diagnosed Parkinson disease. Single Tanta University outpatient center in Egypt; diabetes, atypical/secondary parkinsonism and several comorbidities excluded. 60 randomized, 30 per arm; 27 metformin and 25 control analyzed after withdrawals. The pilot population is narrower than all people with Parkinson disease.


## What exactly was used?

Glucophage metformin tablets, Mina Pharm, Egypt. Oral 500 mg twice daily for 3 months. Both arms received levodopa/carbidopa 250/25 mg three times daily; unnecessary medicines stopped beforehand. Clinical and blood measurements at baseline and month 3. This tests addition to symptomatic treatment, not replacement of that treatment.


## What was the comparator?

Levodopa/carbidopa plus placebo. Parallel allocation, 30 participants per arm. Computer-generated random sequence with permuted blocks; an unblinded pharmacist supplied treatment. Participants and medical professionals blinded; pharmacist not involved in outcome assessment. Recruitment June 2023-August 2024, with 3-month individual follow-up. The report does not fully describe block sizes or every concealment safeguard.


## What was measured and does it matter?

UPDRS improvement primary; TLR4, BDNF, HMGB1 and alpha-synuclein secondary. UPDRS total/subscales, Hoehn-Yahr and Schwab-England; serum ELISA assays. Baseline and 3 months. Clinical symptom/function measures versus circulating surrogate biomarkers. No prespecified minimal clinically important between-group difference is supplied. Disease modification was not directly established by the design.


## How large was the result?

UPDRS median (IQR): metformin 152 (129-166) to 128 (98-151); control 141 (115-164) to 139 (101-151.5). Outcome denominators were 27 metformin and 25 control. Post-treatment rank-biserial correlation r=0.106; between-arm P=0.515 for total UPDRS. Within-metformin P=0.001 does not demonstrate superiority. All listed clinical post-treatment between-arm comparisons were nonsignificant. Confidence intervals for the between-arm clinical effect are not supplied. Table 3 labels all four serum markers ng/mL; reported post-treatment between-arm P values are 0.03 for alpha-synuclein, 0.02 BDNF, 0.03 HMGB1 and 0.04 TLR4. They do not prove clinical benefit.


## What happened to the other observations?

Five control participants needed additional amantadine; two metformin participants changed to combined treatment and one did not attend. Withdrawals were excluded rather than imputed. Pill counts and calls every 2 weeks monitored adherence; seven consecutive days off treatment triggered withdrawal. Clinical deterioration and treatment changes led to outcome-related exclusions. Per-protocol analysis of 52 completers. Individual participants; repeated blood markers and scores are not independent participants. Outcome-related attrition weakens the randomized comparison and may bias either direction.


## What harms were sought and found?

Participants queried for adverse effects at visits and calls every 2 weeks. Table 4 reports 27 metformin and 25 control completers, not all 60 randomized participants. Metformin/control counts: nausea 6/5, diarrhea 4/2, vomiting 5/3, hypotension 9/5, delusions 5/3, abdominal pain 8/5. The described withdrawals mainly involved clinical progression or treatment changes; systematic graded safety data for all randomized participants are not provided. Three months. Nonsignificant safety comparisons in this small completer sample do not establish equivalent safety.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Small single-center pilot, completer exclusions and limited concealment detail. Randomization reduces initial confounding, but post-randomization exclusions and uncontrolled diet/metabolic factors remain. The current NCT05781711 record agrees on 3-month UPDRS and secondary biomarkers; historical versions were not available in this packet. Numerous clinical subscale and biomarker tests without a described multiplicity correction. Small sample and no clinical effect CI limit exclusion of clinically relevant effects. BDNF within-arm P is 0.003 in Table 3 but 0.0003 in prose; baseline ALT/AST P values also differ between prose and Table 1. Preserve table-specific numbers and these discrepancies; do not choose the more favorable P value.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Older adults without diabetes on levodopa/carbidopa at one Egyptian center. One oral dose and one 3-month duration, not all metformin regimens or formulations. Symptom scores and blood biomarkers do not establish slower long-term progression. No demonstrated clinical superiority; external applicability and durability uncertain. A mechanistic rationale remains hypothesis-generating.


## Is this independent evidence?

NCT05781711 / CP016-ARTICLE. The 2016 mouse study is a separate preclinical source, not a companion human trial. One pilot supplies these multiple outcomes; no independent clinical replication is created by multiple citations. Current guide references and the retrieved PubMed/Europe PMC record were checked; no exhaustive clinical search claimed. Keep preclinical support and this small clinical experiment distinct.

## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Parkinson clinical benefit and biomarker hypotheses. Supports reported biomarker differences; does not demonstrate superior clinical UPDRS outcomes or disease modification. The authors acknowledge no significant between-group UPDRS difference; the editorial conclusion agrees and rejects within-arm efficacy inference. Replace vague pending placeholders with this distinction.


## What should this change in our understanding?

This pilot does not establish metformin as an effective Parkinson treatment. Longer-term clinical benefit, tolerability in broader populations and biomarker meaning remain uncertain. Larger blinded trials with intention-to-treat estimates, complete harms and clinically important effect thresholds could change this view. No personal dosing recommendation follows from the pilot.


## Overall appraisal

Clinical superiority was not demonstrated. Outcome-related completer exclusions, short duration, multiple biomarker tests and missing confidence intervals limit interpretation. Biomarker units and table-specific findings are now verified; narrative/table P-value discrepancies remain documented.

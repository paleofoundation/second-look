# COVID-OUT: long COVID prevention after treatment of acute infection

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Evaluate whether early outpatient metformin, ivermectin or fluvoxamine reduces later provider-diagnosed long COVID. Long COVID was added during recruitment in April 2021; the corrected appendix traces its initial primary-outcome listing, later secondary status, and pre-unblinding analysis-plan timing. The relevant claim is prevention of a later diagnosis after acute-infection treatment, not treatment of established long COVID. The evolving endpoint must be described rather than presented as an unchanged original primary outcome.


## Who or what was studied?

Human adults aged 30-85 with overweight or obesity and a recent SARS-CoV-2 infection. Six US sites recruited remotely; symptoms under 7 days, positive test within 3 days, no known previous infection. Pregnancy was permitted for metformin randomization. 1431 randomized; 1323 in the acute modified intention-to-treat population; 1126 entered this long-term analysis, including 564 assigned metformin and 562 matched placebo. The 1126 participants are a selected follow-up population, not all randomized participants.


## What exactly was used?

Oral metformin tablets; the related acute report identifies immediate release, not an extended-release comparison. The studied course used 500 mg on day 1, 500 mg twice daily on days 2-5, then 500 mg morning and 1000 mg evening through day 14. A factorial design included ivermectin, fluvoxamine or their matched placebos; other treatments after enrollment were recorded. Acute follow-up was 28 days; long-term surveys continued to day 300. These are study exposures, not a recommended self-treatment schedule.


## What was the comparator?

Identical metformin placebo, with concurrently randomized controls. Equal allocation to available arms, stratified by site; women pregnant or lactating entered only the metformin/placebo comparison. A pregenerated mass-weighted urn sequence was implemented through a password-protected application; allocation was concealed except from the responsible statistician and pharmacists. Participants, investigators, treating clinicians and outcome assessors were masked; matched pills, opaque packaging and coded packets supported concealment. Recruitment ran December 2020 to January 2022; the factorial arms opened and closed at different dates. Balanced factorial assignments support the metformin contrast; reports of different outcomes remain one trial family.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Long COVID diagnosis was a prespecified secondary outcome by the final pre-unblinding protocol; acute severe COVID was the primary outcome. Participants reported whether a medical provider had diagnosed long COVID and the approximate date; medical records were requested. Diagnoses were assessed through 300 days, with diagnosis questions in surveys from day 180 onward. A clinical diagnosis outcome, not direct enumeration of every persistent symptom or a biological cure. No continuous symptom-scale minimal important difference applies to the binary diagnosis endpoint. Diagnosis depends partly on access to care and recognition; it is not interchangeable with symptom-based long COVID definitions.


## How large was the result?

Table S4 reports day-300 cumulative incidence 6.3% (95% CI 4.2-8.2) for metformin and 10.4% (7.8-12.9) for placebo. Observed diagnoses were 35/564 for metformin and 58/562 for placebo; the observed proportions 6.2% and 10.3% differ from the Kaplan-Meier estimates. The reported absolute reduction is 4.1 percentage points (95% CI 0.9-7.4); unadjusted HR 0.587 (0.386-0.893), rounded in the main Results to 0.59 (0.39-0.89). Between randomized treatment assignments; the adjusted HR is 0.599 (0.393-0.911), not a second independent result. The confidence interval excludes an HR of 1 for this endpoint, but does not establish the same benefit for every subgroup. Use the concordant current Results and corrected Table S4. The Discussion retains a discordant 10.6% placebo value; do not silently erase it or calculate from it.


## What happened to the other observations?

28/564 metformin and 24/562 placebo participants were lost before day 270. 1074/1126 completed at least 9 months or reported a diagnosis; those without a diagnosis were censored at their final completed survey. At least one received dose was required for the original modified intention-to-treat analysis; exact complete-course adherence is not quantified in this long-term report. People never receiving a dose and people not entering qualifying long-term follow-up were excluded from this analysis. Modified intention-to-treat followed by a consenting long-term analysis cohort; it is not a full randomized-population estimand. The individual participant is the unit; repeated surveys and factorial comparisons do not increase independent sample size. Censoring and selection can bias estimates if related to unobserved long COVID risk. Similar measured attrition does not prove missingness was noninformative.


## What harms were sought and found?

Long-term report refers safety ascertainment to the acute companion publication. The 1126 long-term respondents are not automatically the correct denominator for every acute adverse-event analysis. The current registry lists non-COVID adverse events through day 14 and serious events through day 28, with nonsystematic ascertainment for listed nonserious events. It records zero participants with serious drug-related adverse events in all six allocation arms and one all-cause death in the metformin-plus-ivermectin arm; this must not be interpreted as zero treatment risk. Current NCT04510194 results report 14-day all-cause drug discontinuation by six allocation arms: metformin only 56/284, double placebo 49/295, ivermectin only 25/206, fluvoxamine only 32/159, metformin plus fluvoxamine 50/175, and metformin plus ivermectin 29/204. These are allocation-arm counts, not three independent comparisons or all drug-attributable harms. The drug exposure lasted 14 days, whereas diagnosis follow-up extended to 300 days. This prevention report cannot establish comprehensive or long-term drug safety; it also cannot override prescribing-label contraindications.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Masking and concealed allocation reduce some biases; selective entry into long-term follow-up and provider-diagnosis ascertainment remain important concerns. Randomization reduces baseline confounding; adjusted models included other trial drugs and vaccination status. Outcome status changed during the trial, as documented in Table S8; original protocol/SAP versions have not all been directly re-read here. Subgroup estimates are low-powered and numerous; an earlier-start subgroup is not proof that timing modifies treatment effect. Only 93 total diagnoses occurred; subgroup estimates are especially imprecise. Current Discussion says 10.6% placebo incidence, versus Results and corrected Table S4 10.4%. The current registry also lists 59 placebo diagnoses versus 58 in corrected Table S4, with 35 metformin diagnoses in both. The guide uses the identified corrected report estimates and retains these source discrepancies; it does not silently reconcile participant-level differences. A positive secondary outcome is informative but should not be turned into universal prevention advice.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Most directly relevant to early outpatient treatment in adults with overweight/obesity and no known previous infection. The studied immediate-release course does not establish equivalence to extended release or a different duration. Prevention of a provider diagnosis differs from treating fatigue or other symptoms in established illness. Younger adults, normal-BMI adults, recurrent infection, current variants and heavily boosted populations were not all represented adequately. Present the result next to the later prevention and established-illness trials, with their different populations and endpoints.


## Is this independent evidence?

S-COVIDOUT-2023 / NCT04510194. COND-C02-COVIDOUT-2022 reports acute outcomes and MF-COVID-VIRAL-2024 reports viral load from the same trial. These overlapping reports must not be counted as three independent clinical trials. This reconciliation covers the current guide inventory and retrieved publisher records, not an exhaustive new systematic search. The viral-load companion may support mechanism; it cannot independently replicate the clinical prevention outcome.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Carolyn T. Bramante and colleagues, including the COVID-OUT Study Team added by correction. Investigator-initiated multicenter US decentralized trial. Parsemus Foundation, Rainwater Charitable Foundation, Fast Grants and UnitedHealth Group Foundation; investigators also disclose institutional/NIH support. Funders reportedly had no design, data or writing role. Apotex supplied fluvoxamine/placebo and Edenbridge supplied ivermectin/placebo; the report does not identify these donations as metformin supply. The report lists multiple author financial relationships, including consulting, grants and holdings for J. Buse, and consulting for M. Puskarich; it does not say all authors had no conflicts. Disclosures inform scrutiny but neither prove nor disprove the randomized result.

## Which claims does it support or contradict?

C-PREVENT-CO: fewer later provider diagnoses after early acute treatment in this trial. Supports a bounded prevention signal; does not support treatment of existing long COVID, universal prevention, or equivalence of formulations. Observed estimates are distinct from the authors' implementation advocacy; the editorial interpretation retains endpoint, cohort and applicability limits. The guide can cite the corrected-table estimates with an explicit note about the residual Discussion inconsistency.


## What should this change in our understanding?

COVID-OUT supplies a randomized prevention signal in a selected acute-infection population. Benefit for current recurrent-infection populations, symptom-defined long COVID and established illness is not established by this report. Independent contemporary trials with complete follow-up, clear endpoint definitions and prespecified analyses could strengthen or weaken the prevention conclusion. Do not infer an individual treatment decision from this study alone.


## Overall appraisal

The correction is reconciled at the source level. Current Results and corrected Table S4 agree; their estimates can be quoted as reported, with the remaining Discussion discrepancy visible. Selection into long-term follow-up and the changed endpoint history limit interpretation. The current registry safety and discontinuation results were reconciled by allocation arm. All historical protocol versions and participant-level discrepancies have not been independently audited.

# Korean long-COVID treatment trial: no improvement from a two-week course

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test metformin or UDCA for recovery from established PASC. Metformin versus placebo was the powered primary comparison; UDCA was exploratory. Recovery defined by crossing below a symptom-index threshold at week 8. This is treatment, unlike acute-infection prevention trials.


## Who or what was studied?

Adults at least 19 with a previous positive test and PASC index at least 12; median age 36, 72% women, 97% vaccinated. Two Seoul tertiary hospitals, infection 30–730 days earlier; upper limit expanded from 180 days during study. 396 randomized, 132 per arm; abstract says 666 screened and Results says 658. Preserve the screening discrepancy without changing the unambiguous randomized counts.


## What exactly was used?

Oral metformin plus UDCA placebo. Metformin titrated to 1500 mg/day for 14 days; exact formulation and daily steps deferred to inaccessible protocol. Other arm received UDCA 900 mg/day plus metformin placebo; control received both placebos. Visits weeks 1, 2, 4 and 8. Do not infer the formulation from COVID-OUT or another trial.


## What was the comparator?

Double placebo. 1:1:1 parallel allocation. Full sequence generation and concealment details are in the separately linked protocol, unavailable here. Participants, investigators and outcome assessors blinded. Enrollment July 2024–January 2025; follow-up completed April 2025. The report supports randomization but not an invented concealment mechanism.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary PASC recovery at 8 weeks; continuous score, individual symptoms and cytokines secondary. Weighted 12-domain index, range 0–34. Week 8 primary, weeks 2 and 4 additional. Patient-reported symptom index; cytokines are mechanistic surrogates. Recovery threshold below 12 is a classification threshold, not established complete symptom resolution; design targeted 15 percentage points. A participant can cross the threshold while remaining symptomatic.


## How large was the result?

Recovery 84/132 metformin, 90/132 placebo and 90/132 UDCA. Metformin 63.6%, placebo 68.2%. Metformin-placebo difference −4.55 percentage points, 95% CI −15.98 to 6.88. Score change −10.05 metformin versus −10.43 placebo; between-arm difference 0.38 points. Score difference CI −1.42 to 2.18; within-arm improvement is not treatment efficacy. The primary comparison shows no demonstrated benefit, with uncertainty spanning modest benefit and harm.


## What happened to the other observations?

Detailed withdrawal flow and week-8 missing outcome counts require the linked supplement/figure data not fully acquired. Sensitivity analyses for missing week-8 recovery and gray-zone scores described, but exact rules not independently reproduced. Detailed adherence information deferred to protocol. Two placebo participants lacked baseline PASC scores. Intention-to-treat, all 396 randomized, for efficacy; at least one dose for safety. Individual participant; repeated visits and cytokines are not additional people. The common efficacy denominator should not be reused silently for the smaller safety set.


## What harms were sought and found?

Adverse events, serious events, hospitalizations and deaths assessed in those taking at least one dose. Main report gives safety percentages but not all exact exposed denominators; do not infer denominators from rounding. Any AE: 7 metformin (5.4%), 6 UDCA (4.6%), 3 placebo (2.3%); no metformin/UDCA serious events; one unrelated placebo arm fracture; no deaths. Event-specific withdrawals are in unavailable Supplement Table 7. Eight-week follow-up after 14-day treatment. Mostly mild-to-moderate gastrointestinal events; sample and duration limit uncommon-harm detection.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Blinding and randomization strengths; short treatment, selected younger volunteers, uncertain missing-data detail and threshold-based endpoint limitations. Baseline similarity limits measured confounding; selection limits external validity. Full protocol/SAP comparison remains unavailable despite public main-text access. Secondary cytokine and symptom comparisons unadjusted for multiplicity. Placebo recovery 68.2% greatly exceeded assumed 15%; primary CI excludes the targeted 15-point benefit but not smaller benefit. Abstract screening 666 versus Results 658; Discussion describes 70–80% recovery while main ITT recovery is 66.7%. Record these differences rather than silently harmonizing them.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Younger mostly vaccinated adults with generally mild PASC. Two-week oral metformin course only. Week-8 threshold recovery does not establish complete functional recovery or long-term trajectory. Limited applicability to severe, persistent or differently defined long COVID. A null trial of this regimen does not prove all possible regimens ineffective.


## Is this independent evidence?

KCT0009342, independent Korean trial. COVID-OUT and ACTIV-6 are prevention comparators; REVIVE a distinct treatment trial. UDCA and metformin share placebo; do not count shared participants twice. Current guide-source scope only. This contributes one metformin treatment comparison.


## Who conducted and supported it?

So Yun Lim, Jacob Lee, Sung-Han Kim and colleagues. Asan Medical Center and Hallym University Kangnam Sacred Heart Hospital, Seoul. Korean National Institute of Infectious Diseases/National Institute of Health; stated no role in design, analysis or publication. Supplied-product funding details not established in the accessible report. Linked individual disclosure forms were not acquired. Do not convert missing forms into a no-conflict assertion.

## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Established long-COVID treatment. Contradicts a claim that this two-week course improves week-8 recovery. Authors conclude no significant improvement; observed estimates support that bounded statement. Do not substitute preventive trial results for this treatment result.


## What should this change in our understanding?

No demonstrated clinical advantage over placebo for this regimen and population. Longer treatment, severe phenotypes and durable function remain unanswered. Better phenotyping, complete outcome reporting and longer, independently replicated trials could change the conclusion. Do not recommend UDCA or metformin based on improvements shared with placebo.


## Overall appraisal

The randomized primary comparison was null. Substantial spontaneous or nonspecific improvement makes within-arm change misleading. Inaccessible supplement/protocol details are explicitly preserved as gaps and constrain the assessment, without preventing an accurate account of the reported primary result.

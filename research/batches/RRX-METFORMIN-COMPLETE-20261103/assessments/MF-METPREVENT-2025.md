# MET-PREVENT: no walking-speed benefit and poor tolerability in frail older adults

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test efficacy and safety for physical performance in probable sarcopenia with prefrailty/frailty. Primary endpoint specified as adjusted 4-meter walk speed at 4 months. Clinical mobility, not lifespan. The tested group differs markedly from healthy exercise volunteers.


## Who or what was studied?

Adults at least 65, mean 80.4 years; 58% women. Two northeast England NHS trusts; walk speed below 0.8 m/s and low strength or slow chair rise; diabetes and eGFR below 45 excluded. 268 expressed interest, 112 assessed, 72 randomized 36 per arm; 71 exposed, 70 with final data. Probable sarcopenia did not require confirmed low muscle mass.


## What exactly was used?

Aurobindo immediate-release metformin 500-mg tablets. Oral 500 mg three times daily from initiation, without dose titration, for four months. No standardized training cointervention. Outcomes four months; records followed for 28 days after last visit. Poor tolerability may be regimen- and population-specific.


## What was the comparator?

Matching lactose/microcrystalline-cellulose placebo. 1:1 via central web system. Minimization by sex and baseline walk speed with 30% random element. Participants, investigators and assessors masked; identical bottles. Concurrent trial August 2021–September 2022 enrollment. These methods reduce selection and measurement bias.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary adjusted walk speed; strength, function, body composition, quality of life and daily activities secondary. Fastest of two timed 4-meter walks from standing start. Four months. Clinically relevant physical performance. Trial powered for 0.1 m/s difference using its cited meaningful-change estimate. A measured speed difference is more direct than a molecular aging marker.


## How large was the result?

Final means 0.57 m/s metformin versus 0.58 placebo. 34 versus 36 complete cases. Adjusted difference 0.001 m/s, 95% CI −0.06 to 0.06; P=.96. Between-arm difference; per-protocol 19 versus 28 gave 0.02 m/s, CI −0.05 to 0.10. Primary interval excludes the 0.1 m/s benefit targeted in the design. The trial provides evidence against that size of benefit for this regimen.


## What happened to the other observations?

Two metformin participants absent from final follow-up, none placebo. Complete-case ITT; high retention despite medication discontinuation. Median adherence 82% versus 92%; suboptimal adherence more frequent with metformin. 13/34 metformin versus 5/36 placebo discontinued medication but continued follow-up. All randomized for stated ITT; 70 complete cases for primary, 71 exposed for safety. Individual participant, not number of walks or adverse events. Retention and treatment persistence are different measures.


## What harms were sought and found?

Events at each contact, safety blood tests monthly, records through 28 days after last visit. 35 metformin and 36 placebo exposed. 108 events in 35/35 versus 77 in 33/36; hospital admission 12/35 versus 3/36; diarrhea 19/35 versus 9/36. Medication stopped for adverse event in 10 versus 3, and out-of-range safety bloods in 2 versus 1; one metformin death judged unrelated. Four-month treatment plus stated follow-up. Hospitalization imbalance is important but does not prove each admission was caused by the drug.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Strong masking and retention; small trial, low adherence and short duration. Randomization reduces baseline confounding; per-protocol estimates can be selected. Published protocol identified but separate full protocol/SAP and appendix not acquired in this packet. No adjustment for multiple secondary/subgroup tests. Precise enough to exclude targeted primary benefit, not all smaller effects or uncommon harms. Figure 2 shows baseline-speed interaction P=.030 while prose says no significant subgroup differences; retain discrepancy and do not promote the subgroup. Null primary result and tolerability matter more than isolated exploratory findings.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

Very old, predominantly White adults with substantial functional limitation, without diabetes. 1500 mg/day immediate release without titration. Walking, strength and function; not generalized healthspan. Cannot extend directly to younger adults, diabetes care or gradual titration. Does not justify stopping metformin prescribed for another established indication.


## Is this independent evidence?

MET-PREVENT independent RCT. CP018 discusses the planned trial; this is its later completed result. No duplicate participant count via narrative review. Authors searched PubMed through September 2024; this assessment reconciles existing guide sources. Do not present CP018's trial-underway statement as current.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Miles Witham, Claire McDonald, Nina Wilson and colleagues. Newcastle/Gateshead NHS and university collaborators. NIHR Newcastle Biomedical Research Centre; philanthropic mechanistic support from Alan Halsall. ModePharma prepared matched bottles; Aurobindo product identified. Witham disclosed Rejuvenate Biomed consulting; Clegg disclosed grants/roles; funder and sponsor oversaw design/conduct, not analysis or publication decision. No blanket no-funder-role or no-conflict statement is appropriate.


## Is the source current and intact?

Published Lancet Healthy Longevity 2025, DOI 10.1016/j.lanhl.2025.100695. Full ten-page report acquired; primary table and subgroup figure visually inspected. Appendix and separate SAP not present; main flow and safety data support the bounded conclusion. ISRCTN29932357 identified; published protocol DOI 10.1136/bmjopen-2022-061823. No correction/retraction identified in retrieved current records; subgroup discrepancy retained. November 3–4, 2026. This does not claim an independent participant-level audit.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Sarcopenia, frailty and geroprotection claims. Contradicts improvement in walk speed for the tested population and regimen. Authors conclude poor tolerance and no benefit; editorial conclusion is similarly bounded. Retain hospitalization and discontinuation findings alongside efficacy.


## What should this change in our understanding?

No demonstrated mobility benefit, with a substantial tolerability burden. Different doses, titration, populations and durations remain separate questions. Well-powered studies in justified alternative populations with prespecified function and safety outcomes would be needed. Mechanistic anti-aging rationale cannot override this direct trial.


## Overall appraisal

High follow-up retention makes the null primary result informative despite frequent treatment discontinuation. The reported primary interval excludes the targeted 0.1 m/s benefit. Tolerability and hospital admissions require prominent discussion, without attributing every event causally.

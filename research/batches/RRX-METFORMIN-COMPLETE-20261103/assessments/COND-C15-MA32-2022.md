# MA.32: a large negative breast-cancer trial despite metabolic effects

Private source assessment. Independent verification and human approval are pending.


## What question was actually tested?

Test adjunctive metformin for invasive disease-free survival after operable breast cancer. Final primary population hormone-receptor positive after planned futility assessment of receptor-negative disease. Recurrence, new invasive cancer or death. Subgroup analyses cannot replace the primary result.


## Who or what was studied?

Adults 18–74 with high-risk nonmetastatic breast cancer without diabetes. Canada, US, UK and Switzerland; complete resection and standard cancer therapy. 3649 randomized: 1824 metformin and 1825 placebo; primary ER/PgR-positive group 1268 and 1265. Almost all participants were women.


## What exactly was used?

Apotex metformin and matching placebo. Oral 850 mg daily for four weeks then twice daily, intended five years. Standard endocrine, chemotherapy, radiation and targeted therapy as indicated. Median primary follow-up 96.2 months. Not metformin monotherapy for cancer.


## What was the comparator?

Identical-appearing placebo. 1:1 minimization, balanced by center and stratified by receptors, BMI and chemotherapy. Computer-based allocation procedure described as minimization with equal probabilities. Double-blind. Concurrent enrollment 2010–2013, data cutoff October 2020. Randomization and long follow-up are major strengths.


## What was measured and does it matter?

Primary invasive disease-free survival; OS and recurrence endpoints secondary. Centrally verified events and time-to-event analysis. Final follow-up approximately eight years. Clinical cancer outcomes. Design targeted about four percentage points at five years, based on investigator consensus. Metabolic improvements are not the same endpoint.


## How large was the result?

Primary events 234/1268 metformin versus 231/1265 placebo. 18.5% versus 18.3% crude observed event proportions. Incidence 2.78 versus 2.74 per 100 patient-years; HR 1.01, CI 0.84–1.21, P=.93. OS HR 1.10, CI 0.86–1.41; receptor-negative iDFS HR 1.01, CI 0.79–1.30. No primary benefit; exploratory HER2-positive signal lacked a significant HER2-by-treatment interaction. Do not present HER2 subgroup benefit as established.


## What happened to the other observations?

All randomized participants included in assigned-group analyses. Missing dates imputed within known intervals; time-to-event censoring used. Final-year reported continuation 64.3% metformin versus 70.9% placebo in the defined eligible subset. Receptor-negative participants stopped study drug for futility while blinded follow-up continued. ITT, with prespecified/supervised change in primary population. Individual participant. Discontinuation affects exposure but does not create a second trial.


## What harms were sought and found?

All grade 3+ events while taking study drug; treatment-related events thereafter. Full randomized groups 1824 and 1825; exact safety count/percentage reconciliation remains needed. Main text reports 391 (21.5%) versus 328 (17.5%) grade 3+ nonhematologic events; the latter count does not reproduce the stated percentage with n=1825. Toxicity could lead to dose reduction/discontinuation. Intended five years of exposure, with later follow-up. Guide describes greater reported severe toxicity without deriving a precise absolute difference from discordant numbers.


## How could the design or analysis mislead?

Strong blinded large RCT; incomplete drug continuation and changes after interim analysis matter. Randomization protects primary comparison; exploratory subgroups remain vulnerable to chance. A full original protocol/SAP was not recovered. The two acquired metabolic-companion supplements are an interaction table and reporting summary, not the protocol/SAP. Primary alpha .037; secondary and subgroup findings exploratory. CI excludes the originally targeted large relative benefit, but smaller effects remain possible. Abstract diarrhea percentages conflict with Results; placebo grade 3+ count and percentage conflict; no silent numeric repair. Use verified primary outcome rather than discordant harm percentages for precise effect calculations.


## Does this apply to the reader or product?

High-risk operable breast cancer without diabetes. Five-year intended adjunctive regimen. Does not support adding metformin to prevent invasive recurrence. Cannot determine diabetes-specific benefit or every molecular subgroup. Do not use a cancer null result to advise stopping diabetes care.


## Is this independent evidence?

CCTG MA.32 single randomized family. COND-C15-MA32-METABOLIC reports six-month metabolic markers. Companion participants overlap; no extra trial count. Current source reconciliation, not an exhaustive breast-cancer review. Metabolic change is not independent survival replication.


## Who conducted and supported it?

Pamela Goodwin, Bingshu Chen and collaborators. CCTG international trial. Cancer charities and public research grants; CCTG trial sponsorship. Apotex donated metformin and placebo. Multiple investigators disclosed consulting, grants and industry employment/advisory relationships. The main result is assessed from design and data, not sponsor labels.


## Is the source current and intact?

Published JAMA 2022, DOI 10.1001/jama.2022.6147. Full main article acquired; primary table assessed. Full original protocol/SAP not recovered; the metabolic-companion interaction table and reporting summary do not replace it. NCT01101438 identified; no complete historical registry audit. No formal correction located in bounded current search; internal numerical discrepancies retained. November 3–4, 2026. Do not label unresolved source inconsistencies corrected.


## Which claims does it support or contradict?

Recurrence prevention versus metabolic improvement. Strong direct evidence against the proposed large general adjuvant benefit. Author conclusion and primary results agree; favorable exploratory subgroups remain hypotheses. Keep primary and exploratory findings visibly separate.


## What should this change in our understanding?

Metformin did not improve invasive disease-free survival in the primary population. Smaller effects and prospectively defined subgroups remain separate questions. Replicated subgroup-specific randomized evidence with clinical outcomes would be required. Improved insulin or weight measures alone are insufficient.


## Overall appraisal

Large, blinded randomized evidence with long follow-up is negative for the primary clinical endpoint. Metabolic companion results and exploratory HER2 findings do not overturn it. Discordant adverse-event numbers are explicitly retained rather than silently recalculated.
